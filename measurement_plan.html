01What this campaign delivers, and what it does not

Two sessions, one room, two hundred recordings. Session one varies the path: three trajectories against three floor markers at three speeds, walked empty-handed. Session two holds the path and the pace still and varies the human condition instead — an open umbrella at three tilts, a loaded bag, a carried cane, and empty-handed control passes recorded the same day so the comparison is clean. Same mounting, same floor tape, same frozen sensor configuration, so the two sets concatenate into one dataset rather than sitting beside each other.

What is not here is a bicycle or a scooter. Neither can be ridden in this room, and the outdoor half of the campaign is not being run, so this dataset contains no cyclist class at all. The problem it can support is pedestrian versus background, with the carried-object conditions as a robustness axis inside the pedestrian class. That is a narrower problem than the four-class one the getting-started guide sketches, and the report should say so rather than let a reader assume otherwise.

Two sessions in one room are not two locations

The guide asks for at least two locations with one held back entirely as a test set, and the reason is domain shift: a network that has only ever seen one room can learn the room — its clutter floor, its multipath, its noise level — and score well in validation having understood nothing about gait. Recording twice in the same room does not fix that. It captures a different day, not a different site, and calling the second session a second location in the report would be false.

With the outdoor sessions dropped, the honest substitute is a subject-held-out split: train on two people, test on the third, and report it as generalisation to a new person rather than to a new environment. Write that decision into the logbook header before seq 1 of session two, and state the limitation plainly at M3. If a second room becomes available later, one afternoon of recording buys back the stronger claim.

Where the counts land against the guide's targets

The guide asks for at least a hundred passes per class from at least three subjects. Merged into one pedestrian class, session one and session two give 162 passes from three subjects, which clears it comfortably. Kept apart, each of the six condition labels sits between 15 and 87 and none of them clears it — the target applies to the class that gets trained on, not to every label in a filename.

Background is the one genuine shortfall: 38 files against a target of 100. It is also the cheapest thing in the campaign to fix, because background needs no subject and no walk-back — an hour of empty room, HVAC, door and trolley takes would close most of the gap. Note the shortfall now; do not discover it at M3.

02The configuration freeze comes first

Section 6 is blunt about this: recordings made with different sweep or range settings cannot be cleanly mixed into one training set. Every one of the two hundred files in this campaign must carry an identical sensor configuration — both sessions, whatever the gap between them.

That freeze has happened, and session one has been recorded against it. The configuration below was measured on the sensor and written to config/session_config.json, and the Shot Logger checks every recording against it, so drift cannot pass unnoticed. For session two the pilot step is shorter than it was the first time: re-mount, re-tape, run two or three throwaway takes into a pilot/ folder to confirm the room is where you left it, then start the count at one.

Superseded — the first draft of this table was unbuildable

Every value below has now been measured on the XE125 rather than derived on paper, and the original draft failed on hardware four times before running at all. The headline correction: the XM125's exploration server negotiates 2 Mbaud and reports that as its ceiling, giving roughly 200 kB/s. The draft's continuous-sweep configuration wanted about 1 MB/s and produced 399 delayed frames out of 400. Continuous sweep mode cannot work over this link at any useful range, and it would have failed silently — dropped frames produce short files, not errors.

The range is now fixed, and session one is what fixed it

1.00–6.04 m was chosen around this room, and for an indoor campaign that is the right span: the room is six metres deep, the far marker sits inside the beam, and a walker at 1.4 m/s gets about 3.6 s of dwell — several full gait cycles per pass. The argument for extending it was never about this room. It was about giving an outdoor cyclist enough pedal cycles in the beam, and with the outdoor sessions dropped that argument goes with them.

More to the point, the question is closed by facts on disk. Session one is already recorded against this configuration. Changing the range now would orphan those hundred files: session two would no longer share a hash with them, and campaign_report.py would report two configurations, which is the dataset silently splitting in two. The bench procedure in BENCH_CHECK.md still has a job — confirming SNR at the far marker and that the mounting suits the room — but it must not end in a re-freeze.

Two consequences worth carrying into the session. The dataset shrinks to about 0.41 GB for the full 48 minutes of beam-on, so disk space is no longer a real constraint. And throughput is now a settled question rather than a pilot risk — the pilot's job is to confirm the range and mounting suit the room, not to discover whether the link keeps up.

The configuration is written once by tools/freeze_session_config.py into config/session_config.json, and the Shot Logger verifies every recording against it. Do not edit that file by hand; the loader refuses a file that no longer round-trips through the parser, which is what a hand edit usually produces.

03Room setup

Mount the XE125 on the tripod at 1.0 m, matching the crossing-button height the guide specifies, at the closed end of the room with its back to the wall and the beam pointing out along the open axis. The alternative, standing at the open end and firing into the closed end, turns that end wall into a large flat reflector sitting at a fixed range, which costs you receiver dynamic range for no benefit.

Keep the beam parallel to the two side walls. Angling it into a corner is the worst case available to you: a trihedral corner returns energy far more efficiently than a flat wall, and it will dominate the frame.

Run the USB cable backwards, behind the sensor, and keep the tripod legs and everything else out of the region in front. The guide warns that the antenna is sensitive to nearby objects; a cable swinging in the near field puts its own Doppler into every recording of the session.

Tape the floor at 1, 2, 4 and 6 m on the centre line before anyone walks. Subjects follow the tape, not their judgement of distance.

What three walls do to the data

The walls themselves are static, so they sit at zero Doppler and the clutter removal in feature extraction will take them out. That is the easy half.

The hard half is that a moving target also reaches the sensor by bouncing off a side wall. That echo arrives later, so it appears at a longer apparent range, and its radial velocity is the projection along the folded path rather than the direct one. In the spectrogram it shows up as a faint second track shadowing the real one at a different velocity. It is not static, so no clutter filter removes it, and the network will happily learn it as a feature of this room. With every recording in the campaign made in this one room, there is no second site to catch that, which is why it has to be reduced at the source rather than detected later.

You cannot eliminate it indoors. You can reduce it: keep subjects on the centre line, and if the lab has RF absorber, put it at the specular points roughly halfway along each side wall. Whatever you do, note it in the logbook, and note the room's width — a narrow room folds harder.

If the room is under 3 m wide

The diagonal trajectories will not fit at a meaningful angle. Record them at whatever shallower angle the room allows and write the actual angle in the logbook rather than silently recording something closer to a straight approach. Note that crossing paths are out of scope by the guide's own recommendation; diagonal is not crossing.

This session is complete. It is documented here because it fixes everything session two has to match: the mounting, the floor markers, the subject IDs and the configuration hash. Its shot list is plans/locA_indoor_3wall.csv.

Two classes share the hundred, so the split is two thirds pedestrian, one third background — weighted rather than spread evenly, because this room is genuinely better at one of them than the other.

Pedestrian gets the larger share because gait is the signal the whole project rests on and it varies more between people than any other factor. Twenty-two passes per subject is enough to cover three trajectories against three distance markers and three speeds without any cell resting on a single take, which matters because one bad recording in a factor you sampled once silently removes that factor.

Background gets a third, and this is the right room to spend it in. An enclosed space with three walls has a richer and more repeatable clutter environment than open ground: doors, ventilation, furniture, and hard surfaces that produce the multipath discussed above. The guide warns that a classifier which has never seen nothing will confidently hallucinate pedestrians, and indoor background is the cheapest and most varied way to fix that.

The eight standing recordings are the most valuable rows in this table and the reason background grew. A stationary person is background by the guide's class definition, but they are still a body in the beam returning a breathing modulation, and this is the exact case where a pedestrian detector embarrasses itself. Sampling it at three distances means the model sees it at the ranges where it will actually occur.

The operator recordings capture what is happening in all sixty-six pedestrian files anyway — a second person moving just behind the sensor — so the network gets to learn that motion behind the antenna is not a target rather than absorbing it as noise.

The second hundred is recorded in the same room, off the same floor tape, against the same frozen configuration. What changes is the factor. Session one asked where and how fast the person walks; this one asks what they are carrying, and to make that question answerable it holds everything else still: one pace, one line, two directions, two markers.

That is the whole design. Session one's sweep over three speeds and three markers is not repeated here, because repeating it would multiply cells instead of objects — six conditions against nine path-and-speed combinations is fifty-four cells, and a hundred recordings spread over fifty-four cells leaves most of them resting on a single take. Fixing the path buys the depth back: every condition gets five takes per person, and the control gets seven.

Subjects are the same three people under the same IDs as session one — p1 Bernard, p2 Aish, p3 Meghana. Keep the mapping identical in the logbook. campaign_report.py counts distinct subject IDs per class, so a returning person recorded under a fresh ID reports as two people who each did half the work.

Tilt is defined relative to the walker, never to the sensor

Forward means in the direction of travel. On an approach that points the canopy at the sensor; on a depart the identical pose points it away. That asymmetry is intentional — the pose is what the person does, and the geometry column already records which way they were walking.

An operator who helpfully “corrects” the tilt on depart passes so the canopy always faces the sensor collapses umbfwd and umbback into each other, and nothing downstream will notice. Brief the subject once, out loud, before the first umbrella take.

The two empty takes are not padding. This is a different day in the same room, and the furniture, the doors and the HVAC state will not be exactly what they were. Two minutes of empty room is what lets anyone prove afterwards that the clutter floor did not move between the sessions — and if it did, which of the two hundred files are affected.

umbstand is the confuser this class set most needs. A person standing still under an open umbrella is a large bright reflector with no gait at all. Without those two files, a network trained on walkers-under-umbrellas can learn that a big return plus an umbrella means pedestrian, and then fire at someone waiting at a crossing in the rain.

Why six labels rather than a hundred rows of ped

A training script can merge ped, umbup, umbfwd, umbback, bag and canehand into one pedestrian class with a six-line mapping. It can never un-merge them. Put the condition in a note column and it survives exactly as long as the CSV stays next to the recordings; put it in the class slot of the filename and it travels through a folder copy, onto another machine, into someone else's loader. The cost is a longer class list; the benefit is that whether a person under an umbrella counts as a pedestrian stays a decision for whoever trains the network.

Expect campaign_report.py to call all six short against its hundred-per-class target. That target applies to the class you train on, and merged into ped this session contributes 96 passes.

canehand is not cane for the same reason. A mobility cane being used plants and swings at a rate unrelated to the legs, which is what would earn it a class of its own; a cane merely carried is much closer to a bare walker holding a stick. Filing both under one label would put two different signatures in one class. If a subject drifts into planting it in step, stop and re-record — that is a different class and it does not belong in this one.

06Naming, folders and the logbook

Filenames follow the guide's fixed pattern exactly, so the label is readable from the name and no frame-level annotation is ever needed:

Pattern

<class>_<geometry>_<distance>_<speed>_<subject>_take<n>.h5

Session one uses ped and bg. Session two adds umbup, umbfwd, umbback, bag and canehand in the same slot, so umbfwd_approach_6m_normal_p1_take1.h5 parses on exactly the same split. Background reuses the geometry slot for the disturbance source, with na for speed and p0 for subject — so bg_trolley_4m_na_p0_take1.h5 parses on it too.

Neither shot list is hand-written. Both are generated by tools/make_shotlist.py from a YAML plan — session one into plans/locA_indoor_3wall.csv, session two into plans/locA2_indoor_objects.csv. Print the one you are running and tick rows off in the room, or let the Shot Logger tick them for you. The script asserts that every filename in a plan is unique before it writes, which is the failure this protocol is most prone to.

Each session gets its own folder: data/locA_indoor_3wall/<class>/ and data/locA2_indoor_objects/<class>/, one subfolder per class, matching the guide's one-class-per-recording rule. Keep them apart even though the room is the same — filenames repeat between plans by design, ped_approach_6m_normal_p1_take1.h5 exists in both, and the session folder plus the location column are what tell them apart.

The shared logbook needs a header block written once per session — date, room and building, room dimensions, wall materials, which end is open, whether absorber was used, the frozen configuration file name, and the evaluation split decision from section 1 — and then one row per recording carrying only what varies: sequence number, filename, subject, clothing, and anything anomalous. For session two the header also carries the props: umbrella canopy diameter, bag weight, cane length, and which hand carried each. Thirty seconds a recording, as the guide says, and it is what lets you diagnose a problem three weeks later.

07Running order

Both shot lists are ordered deliberately, and both are worth following rather than shuffling.

Background comes first in each, because the empty-room signature has to be captured before anyone has walked through the space and before the furniture moves. After that the two sessions order themselves on different principles, for a reason worth understanding rather than fixing.

Two people run a session: one operates the Exploration Tool and keeps the logbook, one is the target. Rotate so all three subjects get recorded, but never let the person in the beam also drive the laptop.

Session two inverts session one's grouping: the prop stays fixed and the three subjects rotate through it, so each person is called back six times. That is deliberate. Setting up and briefing a condition is the expensive step here, and the alternative — one person doing all thirty-two of their takes in a block — would mean changing the prop eighteen times instead of six, with a fresh chance each time for the umbrella to be tilted the other way round.

Split either session cleanly at the end of a block if you must — never mid-subject, and never across a day if you can avoid it, since the room will not be identical tomorrow. If session two does span two days, re-record the two bg_empty takes on the second day and say so in the logbook.

08Quality checks

Data collection failures are silent. All of these are cheap and all of them have caught real problems in real campaigns.

Both sessions

  • Before seq 1: the evaluation split decision from section 1 — which subject is held out — is written in the logbook header.
  • After the pilot: frame count in a 60 s file matches elapsed time × frame rate. No dropped frames.
  • After every tenth recording: open the last file and look at the spectrogram. A target track should be visible by eye.
  • Every file: size within a few percent of its neighbours of the same duration. An outlier means dropped frames.
  • Every file: sensor configuration in the HDF5 matches the frozen configuration, byte for byte.
  • End of session: re-record bg_empty and compare against the opening one. A changed room signature makes everything between them suspect.
  • Before leaving: all 100 rows of the shot list are ticked and every filename on disk appears in the CSV.

Session two only

  • Before seq 1: the configuration hash reported by the Shot Logger is the same one session one recorded against. If it is not, stop — the two hundred files will not form one dataset.
  • Before seq 1: the first bg_empty take is compared against session one's. A room that has changed a lot is worth knowing about before ninety-six passes, not after.
  • Before the first umbrella take: the tilt convention is briefed out loud — forward means in the walking direction, on approach and on depart.
  • At every prop change: the same umbrella, bag and cane as the previous block, and the weight in the bag unchanged. Recorded in the logbook header, not from memory.
  • During the cane block: the cane is carried, tip clear of the floor. A subject who starts planting it in step is recording a different class — stop and redo those takes.
  • Spot-check one umbfwd approach spectrogram early. A near-empty spectrogram is the expected result there, not a broken recording — but confirm the file itself is intact before recording fourteen more.

People who are not in the team

Subjects are team members only, per the guide's GDPR reasoning. In a room with an open end this needs enforcing rather than assuming — put a sign at the open end for the duration of the session. If someone walks through a recording anyway, delete that file and re-record it; do not keep it as background.
