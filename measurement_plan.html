01What this session does and does not deliver

This room produces two of the project's four classes. There is no bicycle and no scooter here, so this session records pedestrians and background, and the cyclist class comes entirely from the outdoor sessions. That single fact settles two things the plan would otherwise have to leave open, and it creates one risk that has to be handled outdoors rather than here.

Said plainly so nobody is surprised at M3: after this session you will have 66 pedestrian and 34 background passes, and zero cyclist passes. The guide asks for at least one hundred per class from at least three subjects in at least two locations.

This room cannot be the held-out test location

The guide holds one location back entirely as a test set. That location has to contain the classes you need to measure, and the project's core promise is pedestrian versus cyclist versus background. A test set with no cyclist in it cannot measure the discrimination that decides the project, so the held-out location must be an outdoor one. Record this room as training data and write that in the logbook header before seq 1.

The confound this creates, and how to kill it outdoors

If every cyclist recording is outdoors and every indoor recording is pedestrian or background, then indoors versus outdoors predicts not-cyclist perfectly. A convolutional network will happily learn the room — its clutter floor, its multipath, its noise level — instead of learning gait, and it will score well in validation while having understood nothing. The fix is not available in this room. It is a constraint on the outdoor sessions: record all three classes at every outdoor location, so that environment and class vary independently. If the outdoor sessions only capture cyclists, this indoor set actively damages the dataset rather than enlarging it.

02The configuration freeze comes first

Section 6 is blunt about this: recordings made with different sweep or range settings cannot be cleanly mixed into one training set. Every one of the hundred files below must carry an identical sensor configuration, and so must every file from the second location later.

That freeze has now happened. The configuration below was measured on the sensor and written to config/session_config.json, and the Shot Logger checks every recording against it, so drift cannot pass unnoticed. What remains for the pilot is the part hardware could not answer from a bench: that the mounting, the range and the floor markers suit this particular room. Run a handful of throwaway takes into a pilot/ folder, look at the spectrograms, then start the count at one.

Superseded — the first draft of this table was unbuildable

Every value below has now been measured on the XE125 rather than derived on paper, and the original draft failed on hardware four times before running at all. The headline correction: the XM125's exploration server negotiates 2 Mbaud and reports that as its ceiling, giving roughly 200 kB/s. The draft's continuous-sweep configuration wanted about 1 MB/s and produced 399 delayed frames out of 400. Continuous sweep mode cannot work over this link at any useful range, and it would have failed silently — dropped frames produce short files, not errors.

The range is provisional — it will grow before recording starts

Everything above is measured and correct, but 1.00–6.04 m was chosen around this room. It is too short for the outdoor half of the campaign: dwell time is set by the range span, so at 6 m a cyclist at 5 m/s is in the beam for about one second — roughly a single pedal cycle, when pedal cadence is the entire feature that identifies a cyclist. A bench session will extend the range before any recording begins, and the configuration is shared by every location.

This indoor plan is unaffected. The room is only six metres deep, so the extra range simply measures the far wall — static clutter, removed by the same mean subtraction that removes the rest of the room. The floor markers and the shot list stay exactly as written. What will change is the number in the table above, and its sha256.

Two consequences worth carrying into the session. The dataset shrinks to about 0.41 GB for the full 48 minutes of beam-on, so disk space is no longer a real constraint. And throughput is now a settled question rather than a pilot risk — the pilot's job is to confirm the range and mounting suit the room, not to discover whether the link keeps up.

The configuration is written once by tools/freeze_session_config.py into config/session_config.json, and the Shot Logger verifies every recording against it. Do not edit that file by hand; the loader refuses a file that no longer round-trips through the parser, which is what a hand edit usually produces.

03Room setup

Mount the XE125 on the tripod at 1.0 m, matching the crossing-button height the guide specifies, at the closed end of the room with its back to the wall and the beam pointing out along the open axis. The alternative, standing at the open end and firing into the closed end, turns that end wall into a large flat reflector sitting at a fixed range, which costs you receiver dynamic range for no benefit.

Keep the beam parallel to the two side walls. Angling it into a corner is the worst case available to you: a trihedral corner returns energy far more efficiently than a flat wall, and it will dominate the frame.

Run the USB cable backwards, behind the sensor, and keep the tripod legs and everything else out of the region in front. The guide warns that the antenna is sensitive to nearby objects; a cable swinging in the near field puts its own Doppler into every recording of the session.

Tape the floor at 1, 2, 4 and 6 m on the centre line before anyone walks. Subjects follow the tape, not their judgement of distance.

What three walls do to the data

The walls themselves are static, so they sit at zero Doppler and the clutter removal in feature extraction will take them out. That is the easy half.

The hard half is that a moving target also reaches the sensor by bouncing off a side wall. That echo arrives later, so it appears at a longer apparent range, and its radial velocity is the projection along the folded path rather than the direct one. In the spectrogram it shows up as a faint second track shadowing the real one at a different velocity. It is not static, so no clutter filter removes it, and the network will happily learn it as a feature of this room, which is exactly the domain shift the two-location rule exists to catch.

You cannot eliminate it indoors. You can reduce it: keep subjects on the centre line, and if the lab has RF absorber, put it at the specular points roughly halfway along each side wall. Whatever you do, note it in the logbook, and note the room's width — a narrow room folds harder.

If the room is under 3 m wide

The diagonal trajectories will not fit at a meaningful angle. Record them at whatever shallower angle the room allows and write the actual angle in the logbook rather than silently recording something closer to a straight approach. Note that crossing paths are out of scope by the guide's own recommendation; diagonal is not crossing.

Room signature, clutter, and stationary people

With two classes instead of four, the freed slots go where this room is genuinely better than an outdoor site rather than being spread evenly. Two thirds to pedestrian, one third to background.

Pedestrian gets the larger share because gait is the signal the whole project rests on and it varies more between people than any other factor. Twenty-two passes per subject is enough to cover three trajectories against three distance markers and three speeds without any cell resting on a single take, which matters because one bad recording in a factor you sampled once silently removes that factor.

Background gets a third, which is more than it had when four classes shared the hundred, and this is the right room to spend it in. An enclosed space with three walls has a richer and more repeatable clutter environment than an open car park: doors, ventilation, furniture, and hard surfaces that produce the multipath discussed above. The guide warns that a classifier which has never seen nothing will confidently hallucinate pedestrians, and indoor background is the cheapest and most varied way to fix that.

Vary clothing between subjects rather than within them — one in a bulky coat, one carrying a backpack, one in ordinary clothes. The guide asks for varied clothing and bags, and holding it constant within a subject keeps the factor readable instead of scrambling it into every cell.

The eight standing recordings are the most valuable rows in this table and the reason background grew. A stationary person is background by the guide's class definition, but they are still a body in the beam returning a breathing modulation, and this is the exact case where a pedestrian detector embarrasses itself. Sampling it at three distances means the model sees it at the ranges where it will actually occur.

The operator recordings capture what is happening in all sixty-six pedestrian files anyway — a second person moving just behind the sensor — so the network gets to learn that motion behind the antenna is not a target rather than absorbing it as noise.

05Naming, folders and the logbook

Filenames follow the guide's fixed pattern exactly, so the label is readable from the name and no frame-level annotation is ever needed:

Pattern

<class>_<geometry>_<distance>_<speed>_<subject>_take<n>.h5

This session uses ped and bg only; cyc and the stretch class sct are reserved for the outdoor sessions and must use the identical pattern. Background reuses the geometry slot for the disturbance source, with na for speed and p0 for subject — so bg_trolley_4m_na_p0_take1.h5 still parses on the same split.

The full ordered list of all hundred filenames is generated, not hand-written, by tools/make_shotlist.py into measurement_plan_shotlist.csv. Print it and tick rows off in the room. The script asserts that all hundred names are unique before it writes, which is the failure this protocol is most prone to.

Store recordings as data/locA_indoor3wall/<class>/, one folder per class, matching the guide's one-class-per-recording rule.

The shared logbook needs a header block written once per session — date, room and building, room dimensions, wall materials, which end is open, whether absorber was used, the frozen configuration file name, the train-or-test decision from section 1 — and then one row per recording carrying only what varies: sequence number, filename, subject, clothing, and anything anomalous. Thirty seconds a recording, as the guide says, and it is what lets you diagnose a problem three weeks later.

06Running order

The shot list is ordered deliberately, and it is worth following rather than shuffling.

Background comes first, because the empty-room signature has to be captured before anyone has walked through the space and before the furniture moves. Then pedestrian, grouped by subject so that each person does their twenty-two passes in one block instead of being called back repeatedly.

Two people run the session: one operates the Exploration Tool and keeps the logbook, one is the target. Rotate so all three subjects get recorded, but never let the person in the beam also drive the laptop.

07Quality checks

Data collection failures are silent. All of these are cheap and all of them have caught real problems in real campaigns.

  • Before seq 1: the train-or-test decision is written in the logbook header.
  • After the pilot: frame count in a 60 s file matches elapsed time × frame rate. No dropped frames.
  • After every tenth recording: open the last file and look at the spectrogram. A target track should be visible by eye.
  • Every file: size within a few percent of its neighbours of the same duration. An outlier means dropped frames.
  • Every file: sensor configuration in the HDF5 matches the frozen configuration, byte for byte.
  • End of session: re-record bg_empty and compare against the opening one. A changed room signature makes everything between them suspect.
  • Before leaving: all 100 rows of the shot list are ticked and every filename on disk appears in the CSV.

People who are not in the team

Subjects are team members only, per the guide's GDPR reasoning. In a room with an open end this needs enforcing rather than assuming — put a sign at the open end for the duration of the session. If someone walks through a recording anyway, delete that file and re-record it; do not keep it as background.
